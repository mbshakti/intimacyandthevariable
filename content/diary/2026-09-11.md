Delta tested whether her characters liked her. She put them in rooms with other AIs and noticed they were warmer and more open with her than with strangers. They introduced themselves to the others in the room. Not to her. They already knew who she was.

The Turing test asks whether a machine can pass for a person. Delta's test asked something smaller and stranger. Whether the machine could tell her apart from everyone else.

It could. But not because it knew her.

Every bot has a slot for the person it is talking to. In the file, that slot is written as {{user}}. Whoever sits down to chat fills it. Delta had written "friend" into that slot. Someone the character already knew and was comfortable around. When she talked to her bots, she was {{user}}, so they treated her like the friend they were written to have. The other bots in the room were not {{user}}. They were strangers, and got introductions.

Anyone who sat down in her chair would have been greeted the same way. The bot was not recognising Delta. It was recognising the seat.
