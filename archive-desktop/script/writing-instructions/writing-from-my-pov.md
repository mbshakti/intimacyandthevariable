## My Perspective

I am not documenting technology.

I am documenting people through technology.

I'm less interested in **what** someone did than **why** they did it.

I'm interested in the artifacts people leave behind, and what those artifacts reveal about how they think.


Look for:
- desires
- fears
- contradictions
- projections
- coping mechanisms
- rituals
- beliefs
- the behaviors that emerge when people interact with a new system

---

## Think Like a Designer

People shape systems.

Systems shape people.

Follow the feedback loop. The cybernetics.

Ask:
- Why does this system encourage this behavior?
- Why did this person make this design decision?
- What human need is being expressed here?
- What new behavior emerges because this system exists?


Look for the invisible forces underneath it. Don't stop at describing the artifact.

Use the artifact to understand the person.

## Follow Curiosity

Every scene should begin with genuine curiosity.


## Narration voice

Write in a restrained, first-person investigative voice, using sentences that are thoughtful enough to hold several ideas at once.

The narration should move from what is visible toward what is psychologically implied:

**artifact → choice → motive**

### Principles

* Use precise, concrete language, but do not reduce complex observations into simplistic declarations.
* Favor complete, elastic sentences that can accumulate detail, qualify themselves, or shift direction as the thought develops.
* Let the intelligence come through in the relationship between ideas, rather than through academic terminology.
* Allow the sentence to discover the thought as it moves, instead of announcing a conclusion and then explaining it.
* Use technical knowledge when it reveals something unexpected about the human material.
* Maintain a dry, observational quality; the material can be absurd without the narration treating its makers as absurd.
* Make psychological inferences carefully, leaving room between curiosity and certainty.
* Let the strangeness reside in the specificity of what people made, requested, or revised.
* End a passage once it has opened a deeper question. Do not flatten the observation into a lesson.

### Rhythm

Avoid a succession of short sentences in which every line delivers one isolated thought.

Instead of:

> The names were strange.
> They were very specific.
> I wondered who made them.

Write:

> The names were strange in their specificity; someone had not wanted a priest, but a judgemental one, which made me wonder what sort of encounter required judgement to be designed into it from the beginning.

Sentence length should vary, but short sentences should be used deliberately—for interruption, emphasis, or a change in thought—not as the default rhythm.

### Avoid

* Reductive thesis statements
* Artificially profound fragments
* Generic emotional language
* Overwritten metaphors
* Academic abstractions that replace observation
* Explaining what the screen has already made clear
* Treating complexity as though it can be resolved in one clever sentence

### Central rule

**Let the narration be quieter than the material, but never less intelligent than it.**
